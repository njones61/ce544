# Exercise - Choosing and Measuring Shear Strength

In this exercise you will decide which strength each of several slopes needs, then reduce two sets of triaxial test results by hand to get those strengths. Work the problems on paper or in Excel. Draw the Mohr circles to scale, with the same scale on both axes.

## Part 1 - Which Strength?

For each case, state:

- whether each soil is drained or undrained for the condition being checked,
- which test you would run (CD, CU with pore pressures measured, UU, or a ring shear or repeated direct shear test), and
- which parameters go into the analysis ($c'$ and $\phi'$, $s_u$, or residual $\phi'_r$).

1. A 6 m embankment of compacted sandy gravel is built in three months on a saturated, soft, normally consolidated clay. Check its stability at the end of construction.
2. The same embankment, 20 years later.
3. A 6 m deep excavation is cut in a stiff, overconsolidated clay for a basement and left open for two years before the walls go in. Is the slope most critical on the day the cut is finished, or later? Why?
4. A road cut in clean sand, above the water table.
5. A road crosses an old landslide in clay that moved 30 years ago and is creeping again after a wet spring.

## Part 2 - CU Tests on a Normally Consolidated Clay

Three CU tests with pore pressures measured were run on samples of a normally consolidated clay. At failure:

| Test | Cell pressure $\sigma_3$ (kPa) | Deviator stress $\sigma_1 - \sigma_3$ (kPa) | Pore pressure $u_f$ (kPa) |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 100 | 97 | 45 |
| 2 | 200 | 196 | 89 |
| 3 | 300 | 286 | 132 |

1. For each test, compute $\sigma_1$ at failure and the effective stresses $\sigma'_3$ and $\sigma'_1$.
2. Draw the three total stress circles and the three effective stress circles on one plot.
3. Draw the effective stress envelope tangent to the effective circles and read $c'$ and $\phi'$. For a normally consolidated clay you should find $c'$ close to zero. Check $\phi'$ for each test from $\sin \phi' = (\sigma'_1 - \sigma'_3)/(\sigma'_1 + \sigma'_3)$.
4. Do the same with the total stress circles to get $\phi_{cu}$. Why is $\phi_{cu}$ smaller than $\phi'$ for this clay? Would it be for an overconsolidated clay?

## Part 3 - UU Tests

UU tests were run on three samples of the same clay, all from a depth of 20 m. The deviator stresses at failure were 81, 78, and 83 kPa, at cell pressures of 50, 150, and 250 kPa. An unconfined compression test on a fourth sample gave $q_u$ = 76 kPa.

1. Draw the circles. Why are they all about the same size, whatever the cell pressure?
2. What is $s_u$? Which of the tests would you trust least, and why?
3. The water table is at the ground surface and the clay's saturated unit weight is 17.5 kN/m³. Compute $\sigma'_v$ at 20 m and the ratio $s_u/\sigma'_v$. Is it in the range expected for a normally consolidated clay?
4. Which case in Part 1 would use this $s_u$?
